# Chain Reaction

## Overview

*Chain Reaction* is a fast-paced push-your-luck dice game where you act as a reactor operator trying to generate as much energy as possible before your reactor goes into meltdown. Players roll five standard six-sided dice to create a chain reaction of matching numbers, representing the build-up of nuclear energy. The longer the chain (the more matching dice you accumulate), the more energy (points) you generate. However, if your chain reaction falters and you fail to roll a needed match, your reactor suffers a meltdown – you bust and lose all points from that turn. The thrill comes from deciding when to push your luck and when to safely shut down your reactor to bank your points (Game Mechanics: Sometimes You Want to Push Your Luck - The Board Game Design Course). The game's theme is reinforced with fun nuclear terminology (like "meltdown" for bust) to add flavor while keeping gameplay light and exciting.

**Players:** 2 or more (best with 2-5)
**Ages:** 8 and up
**Playing Time:** ~20 minutes
**Objective:** Be the first to reach the target score by building dice chains (matching numbers) to score points, without causing a meltdown (losing your turn's points).

## Components

*   **5 Standard Six-Sided Dice (d6):** These are shared among the players and rolled to create chain reactions.
*   **Score Pad or Paper:** To keep track of each player's accumulated score (energy points) over the game.
*   **Pencils:** For recording scores.
*   **Enhancement Tokens:** One set per player, containing three one-time-use tokens: **Enrichment**, **Control Rod**, and **Fusion**. These tokens represent special abilities (enhancements) players can use to influence the dice rolls. (Alternatively, use coins or markers to signify if an enhancement has been used.)

## Setup

1.  **Prepare the Play Area:** Place the five dice within reach of all players. Give each player a scoresheet and a pencil (or a shared scoresheet with columns for each player).
2.  **Distribute Enhancements:** Give each player one token (or marker) for each enhancement: Enrichment, Control Rod, and Fusion. Players keep their unused enhancement tokens in front of them.
3.  **Set the Target Score:** Decide on the winning score before the game begins. **100 points** is a recommended target for a quick game, but you can choose **200** or more for a longer game. (For an even shorter game, 50 points works.)
4.  **Choose a Start Player:** Select a player to go first (e.g. the last person who watched a nuclear-themed movie, or roll off high die). Play will proceed clockwise.

## Gameplay

The game is played in turns, starting with the first player and proceeding to the left. On your turn, you will roll the dice and try to build a chain of matching numbers. You may continue rolling (pushing your luck) to lengthen your chain and increase your score, or stop to secure your points. But beware: if you roll and no dice match your chain number, your turn ends in a *meltdown* (bust) and you score 0 for that turn (Tabletop Terrors: Take a Gamble with Zombie Dice and Cthulhu Dice from Steve Jackson Games).

**Key Concept – Chain Reaction:** On a turn, you focus on one specific number (1-6) as your *chain number*. You build a chain by collecting as many dice showing that number as possible through successive rolls. Each roll that yields one or more of your chain number *extends* the chain (you add those dice to your chain), and any roll that yields none causes a *meltdown* (bust).

## Turn Sequence

### 1. Start a Chain
Begin your turn by rolling all **five dice**. Look at the results:

*   If you have two or more matching values, choose those values to be your **chain number** for this turn. Gather all dice showing that number and set them aside; this is the start of your chain. The length of your chain is the number of dice set aside.
*   If you roll multiple matching sets (e.g., three 2s and two 6s), you must choose which number will be your chain number. You cannot use both. While it might seem obvious to take the larger set, sometimes the smaller set of higher numbers is strategically better. For example, if you roll 2, 2, 2, 6, 6, choosing the two 6s gives you 12 points (6×2) to start versus only 6 points (2×3) for the three 2s. Plus, you'd have three dice left to roll for more 6s rather than just two dice for more 2s. Both probability and scoring potential matter in this decision.
*   If you rolled **no matching values** (i.e. all five dice show different numbers), your reactor suffers a **Radiation Leak**. Your turn ends immediately, and you lose 10 points from your total score. This penalty represents an unstable, leaky reaction with no viable fuel source to begin the chain. (9.26% chance of this happening on first roll)
*   *Typical Example:* You roll 5, 2, 5, 3, 5. You have three 5's. You decide 5 will be your chain number. Set those three 5s aside – your chain starts at length 3.

### 2. Continue or Stop
After establishing your chain, assess the situation:

*   If **all five dice** are now in your chain (i.e. you managed to roll five of a kind right away), you've achieved **Critical Mass** – the reactor is maxed out. Skip to scoring (you'll score for a chain of 5 and end your turn, since no dice remain to roll).
*   Otherwise, you have some dice set aside in your chain and some dice remaining that are not in the chain. **You must now decide: either stop or continue:**
    *   **Stop (Voluntary Shutdown):** End your turn and score points for the current chain length (see ## Scoring below). This is the safe move to lock in your gains.
    *   **Continue (Push Your Luck):** Keep the reaction going! Roll all the dice that are *not* in your chain (the ones you haven't set aside yet) to try to extend your chain.

### 3. Roll to Extend the Chain
If you chose to continue, roll all remaining dice (any dice not set aside in the chain). After the roll, evaluate the outcome:

*   If **one or more** of the dice show your chain number, congrats – the chain reaction continues! Add all dice showing the chain number to your chain, increasing its length. Set these matching dice aside with the rest of your chain. Any dice that rolled your chain number are now part of the chain and won't be rolled again this turn.
*   If you roll a **new chain** (two or more matching dice of a different number), you may choose to:
    *   **SWITCH:** Discard your current chain and start a new chain with the newly matched dice, OR
    *   **KEEP:** Keep your current chain and add any matching dice of your current chain number (if any were rolled).
    This gives you the opportunity to "upgrade" your chain if you roll a more valuable set (e.g., switching from two 2s to two 5s).
*   If **none** of the dice show your chain number AND you don't roll a new chain (or choose not to switch), the chain reaction fails – your reactor has hit a **Meltdown**. A meltdown is essentially a *bust*: your turn ends immediately and you score **zero points** for this turn (any points you had accumulated this turn are lost). In other words, you pushed too far and your risky roll wiped out your progress (Tabletop Terrors: Take a Gamble with Zombie Dice and Cthulhu Dice from Steve Jackson Games). (This *does not* make you lose points scored in earlier turns; it only affects the current turn's unscored points.) We call this a meltdown because in nuclear terms, the core overheats and melts down when the chain reaction can't be sustained or controlled (Nuclear meltdown - Wikipedia).
*   If you did roll matches and extended the chain (or switched to a new chain), you now again check: have you used all 5 dice in the chain yet? If not, you again face the choice to **stop** and score, or **continue** with another roll of the remaining dice. Repeat the cycle of **roll -> match (or meltdown) -> decide** until either you stop or you meltdown.

### 4. Repeat or End Turn
Continue rolling and extending your chain as long as you dare, until either:

*   You decide to **stop** and score your chain, or
*   You suffer a **meltdown** (bust) and end the turn with no points from this turn.

After either outcome, play passes to the next player clockwise.

**Push-Your-Luck in Action:** After every successful roll, you'll weigh the **risk vs reward**. Stopping yields guaranteed points; continuing could increase your score or wipe it out. This is the heart of the push-your-luck experience – deciding whether to "press" for more or "cash out" now (Game Mechanics: Sometimes You Want to Push Your Luck - The Board Game Design Course). Experienced players learn to gauge probabilities (e.g. rolling 2 dice to try to get at least one matching a certain number) versus the potential reward for adding one more die to the chain.

**Example of a Turn:**
Alice chooses **3** as her chain number (she rolled 3, 3, 6, 1, 5 on her first roll, so she has a chain of two 3's). She has 3 dice remaining. Alice decides to continue. She rolls the 3 remaining dice and gets **5, 5, 2**. She now has a choice: keep her chain of two 3's (worth 6 points) OR switch to a new chain of two 5's (worth 10 points). Alice decides to switch to the 5's since they're worth more points. She now has a chain of two 5's and one die remaining. Alice continues and rolls the last die... She gets a **5**! Her chain is now three 5's (worth 15 points). Alice decides to stop and scores 15 points for her turn.

## Enhancements

To add strategic depth and a thematic twist, each player has access to three one-time Enhancements that can alter the outcome of rolls. Each enhancement represents a nuclear technology trick to influence the chain reaction. A player may use at most **one enhancement per roll** (you can't stack multiple on the same dice roll), and each can only be used **once per game** (discard the token after use). These enhancements are optional tools – you don't have to use them, but they can save you from disaster or help you push for a higher score at critical moments. Use them wisely, as each is single-use!

The three enhancements are: **Enrichment**, **Control Rod**, and **Fusion**. Here's what each does:

*   **Enrichment (Fuel Boost):** You inject enriched fuel into the reaction to boost it. *After any roll*, you may change the face of **one die** to match your chain number. This can turn a failure into a success or simply add an extra matching die to your chain. For example, if you rolled and got only one die showing your chain number, you could use Enrichment to change one of the non-matching dice into that number as well, effectively getting an additional match. If you rolled no matches (which would cause a meltdown), you could use Enrichment to change one die into the chain number, preventing the meltdown. Thematically, this represents adding enriched uranium to keep the chain reaction going (enriched uranium has a higher proportion of fissile U-235 to sustain reactions (Enriched uranium - Wikipedia)). Once used, discard your Enrichment token.

*   **Control Rod (Emergency Shutdown):** You deploy control rods to prevent a catastrophe. *If a roll results in no matches* (a impending meltdown), you may immediately activate Control Rod to stop the meltdown. The Control Rod halts your chain reaction safely. **Effect:** Your turn ends immediately at that point but you get to **score the points you had accumulated in your chain *before* that roll**. In other words, you avoid losing your points to a meltdown by performing an emergency shutdown of the reactor. Use this when you've pushed a roll too far – it's your safety net. For example, if you had a chain of length 4 (worth a lot of points) and then rolled and got no matches, normally you'd bust and get 0, but with Control Rod you can save that chain's score (though you don't get to continue rolling). Thematically, control rods absorb neutrons to slow/stop the nuclear chain reaction (Control rod - Energy Education), preventing an out-of-control reaction – just like how this ability stops a bust from happening. Discard your Control Rod token after use. (You cannot continue rolling after using Control Rod; it is a shutdown procedure.)

*   **Fusion (Combine Dice):** You trigger a fusion reaction, combining two different chains into one massive energy release. *After a roll*, if you have **at least two matching dice of a different number** than your current chain (e.g., you've already chained 5s and then roll two 6s), you may use Fusion to **end your turn** and merge the two sets for scoring. This is the only enhancement that allows scoring from more than one number.
    *   *For example*, if your chain was two 5s and your next roll gave you two 6s, you could stop and use Fusion to score both sets: 5×2 + 6×2 = 10 + 12 = 22 points. You **must stop** your turn immediately when you use Fusion. You cannot continue rolling afterward.
    *   This adds a layer of timing and value judgment: you'll want to save Fusion for when both sets are high-value (e.g., 5s and 6s), and using it on low numbers like 1s and 2s may not be worth it. Fusion is a high-reward move when deployed at the right moment. Discard your Fusion token after use.

### Enhancement Usage Notes:

*   You may use an enhancement at the appropriate moment *after* you roll but *before* you resolve the outcome. For **Enrichment** and **Fusion**, use them immediately after rolling, to alter dice results before determining if you busted or not. For **Control Rod**, use it immediately after a bust roll is seen (to retroactively avoid the bust).
*   **Only one** enhancement can be used on a single roll. For instance, if you roll a bad result, you must choose either Fusion or Enrichment (if applicable) *or* use Control Rod – you cannot, say, use Enrichment and then also use Fusion on the same roll.
*   Each enhancement can be used **once per game**. After you use it, remove the token from play (or flip it over to show it's spent). Plan carefully when to deploy them; they can swing the game at critical moments.
*   If you never need or choose to use an enhancement, that's fine – using them is optional. Some players may save them for emergencies or high-risk moves. Unused enhancements simply don't score anything extra at game end or anything; they're just one-time abilities.

## Scoring

Whenever you end your turn by stopping voluntarily or by a controlled shutdown (using **Control Rod**), you score points based on the length of your chain (the number of dice in your chain). Longer chains yield more points. If you ended your turn in a **meltdown** (bust without using Control Rod), you score **0** for that turn.

**Energy Points Scoring:** Your score is calculated by multiplying the **chain number** by the **number of dice** in your chain.

*   *Example:* Five 3s = 3 × 5 = 15 points
*   *Example:* Four 6s = 6 × 4 = 24 points
*   *Example:* Two 1s = 1 × 2 = 2 points

This scoring method keeps math simple and emphasizes both building longer chains and targeting higher-value numbers. It rewards both quantity and face value, making risk-taking decisions more nuanced and thematic.

Record the points you scored on your scoresheet and add it to your total from previous turns. If you used **Control Rod** to end your turn, score the points for the chain length you had *before* that last roll (e.g. if you had 4 dice in chain before the bust, you score the 4-dice chain value). If you busted without Control Rod, score nothing for that turn.

*Example:* Jen stops with a chain of four 4s. She scores 4 × 4 = 16 points. Mark manages to get a 5-dice chain of 5s – that's 5 × 5 = 25 points for him. Leo had a chain of three 6s (6 × 3 = 18 points) but decided to push for a fourth; unfortunately, he rolled no match and busted, so he scores 0 for that turn (a tough break!). Nina had a chain of two 2s (2 × 2 = 4 points) and decided to keep going. She busted on her next roll, but since the score at risk was low, the loss wasn't too painful..

All players accumulate points over their turns. Keep a running total for each player.

## Winning the Game

Keep taking turns, with players accumulating points, until at least one player's total score reaches the agreed target score (or exceeds it) **at the end of a turn**. That player is declared the winner - they've generated enough energy to win the race! For a standard game, the target is **100 points** (feel free to adjust the target higher or lower for longer or shorter games).

If multiple players exceed the target in the same round (for example, Player A crosses 100, then Player B on their turn also crosses 100 with a higher total), the one with the **higher score wins**. In the unlikely event of an **exact tie**, play additional rounds until the tie is broken.

**Optional rule for fairness:** If one player reaches the target, you may allow each of the other players one final turn to try to catch up or surpass the leader (since not everyone gets an equal number of turns in a game). After those final turns, whoever has the **highest score wins**.

Celebrate the winner as the master of the reactor, and perhaps encourage them to yell "Critical Mass!" or do a victory dance (radiation suit not required). Then, you can reset all scores and play again!

## FAQ

**Q:** If a player gets a **Radiation Leak** on the first roll, can they use an enhancement to prevent losing points?
**A:** Yes, a player can use **Enrichment** to change one die to match another, creating a pair and avoiding the Radiation Leak penalty. **Control Rod** and **Fusion** cannot help in this situation since they only work after you've established a chain.

**Q:** Can I change my chain number during my turn?
**A:** Yes, if you roll a new chain (two or more matching dice of a different number), you can choose to switch to this new chain or keep your current one. Once you decide to keep or switch, you must stick with that chain until you roll another new chain.

**Q:** What happens if I roll all five dice showing the same number on my first roll?
**A:** Congratulations! You've achieved instant **Critical Mass**. Score points for your five-of-a-kind (number × 5) and end your turn. *[See Clarification Below - Build Instructions modify this rule slightly]*

**Clarification based on Build Instructions:** The Build Instructions state that after scoring an initial Critical Mass, the player does *not* end their turn but immediately rolls all 5 dice again to start a *new* chain attempt within the same turn. The FAQ answer reflects the base PDF rules, while the implementation follows the specific Build Instructions.

**Q:** Can I use more than one enhancement in a turn?
**A:** You can use **one** enhancement **per roll**, but potentially multiple different enhancements on different rolls within the same turn.

**Q:** If I use **Control Rod** to prevent a meltdown, do I still get to roll again?
**A:** No. Using Control Rod ends your turn immediately but allows you to score the points accumulated before that roll.

**Q:** What happens if the game ends in a tie?
**A:** If multiple players reach or exceed the target score in the same round with exactly the same total, play additional rounds until the tie is broken.

**Q:** Does the **Fusion** enhancement work with any two sets of matching dice?
**A:** Fusion only works if you have at least two matching dice of a **different** number than your current chain number. You must use it immediately after the roll that produced this second set. Your turn immediately ends and you capture the points of the sets forming pairs.

**Q:** If I roll five different numbers on my first roll (**Radiation Leak**), do I still get to take a turn after the penalty?
**A:** No. Your turn ends immediately after taking the 10-point penalty.

**Q:** Can enhancements be used to help other players or only yourself?
**A:** In the standard game, enhancements can only be used on your own turn. The Cooperative Mode variant allows players to use enhancements to help others.

**Q:** If I have all five dice in my chain and then use the Critical Mass Chain variant to roll again, can I choose a different chain number for my second attempt?
**A:** Yes. After scoring your first chain of five dice, you start a new chain and can choose any matching number from your new roll.

**Q:** What happens if my score goes below zero due to a Radiation Leak penalty?
**A:** Your score cannot go below zero. If you have fewer than 10 points and suffer a Radiation Leak, your score becomes 0.

**Q:** Can I voluntarily end my turn after just the first roll, even if I have dice remaining?
**A:** Yes, you can choose to stop and score after any successful roll, including the first one.
