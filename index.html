THE OPERATOR’S HANDBOOK

Good rolls. Better decisions.

Roll five dice. Choose any matching group of two or more as your chain. Bank face value × dice count, or roll the remaining dice to grow it.

  1. Choose your pairing. On 2, 2, 2, 6, 6, you can take three 2s for 6 points or two 6s for 12. Neither group is selected for you.
  2. Keep or switch. A later roll can extend your chain, create a different pair, or do both. Choose which to keep. Switching gives up the old chain's points and returns its dice to the rolling pool. Keep the new chain and choose whether to bank or roll the other dice again.
  3. A miss is a real risk. Without a matching die, you must switch to a new pair, use a legal enhancement, or accept a meltdown. You cannot bank the old chain after a miss just by choosing “keep.”
  4. Know the endings. A meltdown scores zero for this turn. Five different dice on the opening roll cost 10 points, with a floor of zero. Five matching dice automatically score and end your turn. Switching to a new pair or triple keeps your turn going.

Three ways to change your luck

Enrichment changes one available die to your chain value. Before choosing an opening chain, choose the die and an existing face value to form or improve a pair.

Control Rod banks your pre-roll chain after a roll with no matching dice. It ends your turn.

Fusion banks your current chain (including new matching dice) plus a different newly rolled pair. It ends your turn. On an opening split, select a chain first, then fuse the other pair.

Each ability is once per player per game. Only one ability per roll. Choose or use an ability before rolling again or ending the turn.

The final round

When someone reaches the target, finish the current round so everyone has equal turns. Highest score wins. Tied leaders alone play extra full rounds until one leads. No extra tokens.

Read the risk

The survival percentage includes either matching your chain or rolling a replacement pair; it excludes enhancements. Surviving by switching can still reduce your points. With linear scoring, banking is often the strongest choice, especially with just one die left.
